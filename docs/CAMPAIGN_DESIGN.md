## Unified game identity

**Genre:** original photorealism-oriented first-person action experience with (1) **Story Campaign**, (2) **Multiplayer Operations** (team-based), and (3) **Battle Royale / Extraction-adjacent survival events**. Campaign and online modes share a grounded fictional world, movement, visual language, audio, systems and performance settings, but feature different pacing and fairness rules.

**Central hook — THE SIGNAL / 잔향 지도:** In the fictional Middle Eastern **사히르 연안 (Sahir Coast)**, a protracted civil conflict collides with the collapse of the civil communication grid. The player can reconstruct *limited, fallible* traces of lost broadcasts, sensor logs and civilian witness accounts to discover changing routes, verify misinformation and reveal hidden map history. The system is **not magic, mind-reading or a real-world signal exploitation tutorial**; treat it as a readable, fictional investigation-and-level-state mechanic. Clues matter because they change the next playable **route, objective, support, timed map event or ally response**, not because players are forced to read walls of text.

**Core distinction:** The map has **two simultaneous layers**: the visible immediate environment and a dated, imperfect account of what happened there. The game's dynamic objective network gives combat/movement, investigation and environment interaction a coherent narrative reason; optional traces reward attention while the main mission never soft-locks on a clue hunt.

**Tone:** Fast, cinematic and surprising, yet credible and ethically balanced. Fictional countries and factions rather than copies of current armies, identifiable real-world groups, logos, events or real civilian suffering. Avoid glamorising real victims or stereotyping Middle Eastern peoples. Different perspectives are presented with humanity and agency. No graphic gore.

|---|---|---|---|
| Call of Duty | memorable story set pieces, responsive controls, clear transitions between modes | timeline-sensitive missions whose earlier player decisions transform later spaces and character trust | no recognisable mission recreations, operator roster, cinematic shot-for-shot sequences, UI, logos or narrative arcs |
| Grand Theft Auto | city atmosphere, incidental systems, reactive environment and characters | dense **bounded hubs** with interconnected civilian, infrastructure and faction state; no promise of a giant open world | no copied city, characters, wanted-star logic or satirical mission beats |
| PUBG | route decisions, readable space, shrinking hazard and fair match tension | BR with a **shifting information grid** and map events announced with adequate notice; parity of information between players | no copied map, UI, loot economy, drop plane or named locations |
| Bodycam | camera presence, material/lighting credibility, tactile ambience | original first-person camera grammar with user-configurable shake/headbob, photogrammetry-inspired surfaces and clear accessibility safeguards | no exact bodycam filter/look replication or protected assets |

Avoid a feature soup: **only share systems when they improve all modes** and otherwise keep modules separate. A finished, accessible product wins over copied spectacle.

## Campaign narrative premise

In the fictional Sahir Coast city of Nahrin and its surrounding districts, a ceasefire breaks down just as the **VEILPOINT** civic archive and communications backbone goes dark. Rival authorities claim conflicting responsibility. The player controls **이수혁 대위 (Lee Su-hyeok)**, a fictional Republic of Korea special-forces captain, supported by fictional **국정원 agents 김지윤 and 조현지**, military squadmates, and independent local partners **마라 다한 (Mara Dahan)**, **유수프 라할 (Yusuf Rahal)** and **나디아 파리스 (Nadia Faris)**. A small group of civilians, mechanics, relief personnel and commanders are fully realised voices, not generic props. Across desert transit belts, the coastal capital, mountain relays and a damaged port, seemingly contradictory transmissions reveal that the fracture is being amplified by a system intended to save lives. No faction is written as intrinsically heroic or villainous based on nationality or religion.

**Dramatic escalation:** local rescue / unreliable orders → contested routes → city-scale blackout → divided loyalties → a public truth that can be revealed imperfectly → final evacuation with lasting consequences. Consequences affect scene state, relationships and the epilogue; avoid fake choices where nothing changes.

### Pace and intensity without monotonous noise

**Design requirement:** No long *unintentional* boredom, but also no 12 continuous hours of maximum stimulation; constant peaks flatten drama, induce fatigue and destroy surprises. Plan each chapter as a repeating but **varied** sequence of 3–7 minute micro-goals, interspersed with short active discovery and character beats. Each 15–20 min should offer at least one meaningful new **interaction, spectacle, location logic change or high-consequence reveal**; no arbitrary explosions or intrusive retention tactics. Build quiet scenes with agency (navigation, interpretation, conversation, environmental storytelling) rather than unskippable exposition.

**Encounter diversity:** time-limited escapes, chase/set-piece traversal, branching route choices, dynamic weather/lighting, safeguarding people, environment systems, search/rescue, observation, puzzles with an accessible bypass, tense navigation and competitive encounters. No two consecutive chapters should have identical primary verbs. Introduce mechanics once, remix them twice, then evolve them. Important narrative scenes get clear save/checkpoint and replay access.

**Accessibility:** explicit camera motion sliders, field of view, subtitles, colour and audio cues, adjustable challenge, pausable single-player, skippable previously seen cinematics, content warnings, no mandatory rapid tapping; limit flashing effects.

## Shared gameplay systems and mode differences

- **Single-player campaign:** authored missions, real character arcs, reliable checkpoints, multiple paths, localized states and AI. Main objectives remain discoverable even when a player ignores signal clues.
- **Multiplayer Operations:** original round-based objective competitions and optional collaborative mission events, authoritative server, transparent team communication controls, anti-cheat and carefully balanced player information. Signals become public, contestable **map-state information**, not an invisible pay-to-win mechanic.
- **Battle Royale:** inherited survival core; a fair, shrinking/reshaping play region with a dynamic information grid that can alter routes or reveal safe transit lanes. Announce events in advance and test that newcomers can understand them.
- **Cross-mode continuity:** common controls, renderer, graphics presets, materials, audio identity, shared map chunks and interaction interfaces; separate progression, scoreboards and matchmaking. **No character power carried into competitive modes** and no gambling-style rewards or coercive retention.

### 2027-04-27 full-production stretch

The complete first-play 12h campaign (12 distinct, fully playable chapters) **plus** online Multiplayer Operations **plus** Battle Royale, photoreal visuals and actual 165Hz support is an ambitious target and requires engine builds, original/licensed assets, narration, environmental art, audio, AI, server deployment, playtesting and significant human production. A solo developer may need substantially more time. Never convert a document or mission data file into a false completion claim.

**Gates for a finished campaign:**
1. All 12 chapters built, selectable/unlockable, playable start-to-finish with checkpoints and autosave.
2. Narrative choices produce demonstrable gameplay/world changes; no broken critical path.
3. Target first-play time measured on multiple real players (median near 12h, plausible 10–14h range). Sum of planned chapter minutes is **not measured runtime**.
4. Distinct scene layouts, authored assets, VO/subtitles, spatial audio, UI, cinematic transitions, 100% critical-path playtest.
5. Accessibility, difficulty options, performance, persistence and packaging validated.
6. Independent originality review: no copied missions, maps, characters, logos, textures, UI, protected dialogue or recognizable sequence of major beats from reference games.

**Immediate engineering priorities:** (P0) write data-driven chapter metadata and deterministic campaign progression/checkpoint tests; (P0) implement a playable intro vertical slice and mode select; (P1) build branching signal map/state transitions shared safely across modes; (P1) authored environment prototypes; (P1) actual native UE build and multiplayer validation when possible.
